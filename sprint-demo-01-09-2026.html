Memory Profiles Become Their Own Data TypeRolling Out

See and control what memory profiling costs you, separately from CPU.

Before

Memory-allocation profiles rode inside cpuProfiles. One line covered both, so a customer could not tell what memory profiling cost and could not budget it on its own.

→
Now

memoryProfiles is its own entityType, charged the same way as continuous profiling profiles. It is metered, billed and enforced, and it has its own line in Data Usage.

Value

Continuous profiling stops being one undifferentiated line. Customers can see, budget and block memory profiling on its own, instead of guessing what it costs inside CPU.

AI Gets Its Own Units PlanRolling Out

AI runs on its own units plan, separate from the CX units that cover telemetry.

Before

Every AI capability had its own usage line. Olly sat in the entity-type list, evaluations and guardrails were counted somewhere else, and there was no single AI limit to allocate or enforce.

→
Now

One AI Units plan covers every AI capability, in its own allocation section on Quota Rules. Olly is no longer an entity type.

Value

Two plans, priced separately. CX units cover telemetry, AI units cover AI, and units do not move between them. AI spend cannot quietly eat the telemetry budget, and AI stops being sold as a bolt-on.

RUM Gets Its Own Cost ControlsRolling Out

Price RUM on its own terms, instead of whatever your logs policy happens to be doing.

Before

RUM rode the same TCO policies as logs. Tuning log cost moved RUM cost as a side effect, and browser and mobile traffic could not be priced on its own terms.

→
Now

RUM has its own TCO policies, separate from logs and on by default, with Terraform support alongside the UI.

Value

Customers control RUM spend without touching their log strategy. RUM stops being a cost that moves when nobody was looking at it, which is the precondition for selling RUM at volume.

Span Policies, Written in DPXLShipped

Write a span cost policy in the same language you already use for logs.

Before

Span policies were authored in their own editor with their own rules. Anyone already fluent in DPXL for logs had to learn a second way to say the same thing.

→
Now

Spans use the DPXL policy editor, with quota-triggered priority demotion behind it. One expression language across logs and spans.

Value

One language means one skill. A customer already writing DPXL for logs controls span cost on day one, with no second learning curve and no second place to get it wrong.

DPXL Quick Action AssistantShipped

Take the expression you are looking at straight into Explore.

Before

You read a DPXL expression on a policy, then retyped it by hand into Explore to find out what it actually matched. Most people did not bother.

→

Value
