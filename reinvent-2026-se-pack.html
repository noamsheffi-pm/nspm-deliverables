Demo 1 · Usage & Quota Overview · Live

Data stops, and nobody
can say why.

What the day looks like now

Before this page, data stopped and nobody in the app could say which quota caused it, when it started, or when it lifts.

Quota is enforced in four places: quota rules, TCO quota, dataset quota, team quota. The settings sit on different screens.

When the shared pool ran out, the app said nothing. The customer learned it from email or Slack.

Why the obvious fix fails

A usage chart shows how much was used. It does not say which limit fired, or when data flows again.

Open with

“When a team of yours stops sending data, how do they find out, and how long does it take?”

Ask the opening question before you show anything. Let the visitor tell their own story first. That story is your demo script. Do not lead with the page.

Demo 2 · Monthly plan · Live, on request

They size for the busiest day
and pay for it every day.

What the day looks like now

A daily plan is enforced every single day. So the customer sizes for their busiest Monday, or they get blocked on it.

Every quiet day is quota they paid for and did not use.

Why the obvious fixes fail

Buy for the peak: they pay for the peak all month.

Buy for the average on a daily plan: they get blocked on the day they most need the data.

Open with

“Does your data volume look the same on a Tuesday and on a Saturday? What did you size for?”

This is the easiest pain in the pack to make a visitor feel. Once they say their weekend is quiet, they have made your argument for you.

Demo 2 · The idea and the proof

Commit to your month,
not to your worst day

1

The month is the budget. Daily rate multiplied by the days in that month. February and March differ.

2

One day can go to three times the daily rate. We call it the daily burst. It stops one incident from eating the month.

3

Warn before the limit, block at it. The burst is enforced, not advisory.

4

When the month is used up, the account team can add units. Do not promise pay as you go on monthly.

Our first monthly account

Daily quota in the contract: 180 units

October average: 64 units a day

Busiest day in October: 133 units

On a daily plan, 180 was the cap every day. In October they used about a third of it on average.

Guardrail

Do not name the account. Say “one of our customers”. Do not show or claim a block on a real account.

Show the Usage and Quota screen with the three numbers: daily pace, monthly for this month, daily burst. If no demo environment is on monthly, tell the story with the slide and offer a follow up call. SE advice from the 12-11 handover, not a policy: size on a percentile such as P80, not on an average.

Demo 2 · Hard questions

What they will ask

QuestionAnswer
What exactly is the daily burst?Three times the daily rate, in one day. It protects the customer from spending the month on one spike, and it lets us plan capacity.
Can I change it myself?Not today. We set it per team, 3x by default. A customer setting is being built. No date.
I size wrong and run out on day 29. Then what?The account team can add units. Pay as you go on monthly is not available yet. That is why sizing matters.
I add units mid month. Full month or the rest?Not final yet. Bring the case to us.
Does the burst apply to AI units?No. AI units are a separate monthly budget with no burst.
Can I carry unused units, or go over a little?Not today. Monthly has no carryover and no overage grace. Raise this before a customer switches.
How do I switch a customer?It is on request today, not open to everyone. Bring the account to us.
Two traps here. First, do not call the burst a soft limit: it blocks. Second, do not promise carryover, spillover, quarterly or yearly enforcement. The honest line is that we cannot enforce what is not modeled in the contract system yet, and that work is underway.

Demo 3 · Traffic Management · Preview

Every event gets
the same treatment.

What the day looks like now

One fixed pipeline: parse, TCO, enrich, categorize, store. In that order, for everything.

So you cannot keep a full copy for compliance and a trimmed copy for daily use. You pick one.

A business unit cannot own its own budget and see its own traffic.

Nobody can see the pipeline at all.

Why the obvious fixes fail

More TCO rules: each rule caps one application or subsystem. There is no shared limit for a business unit.

More Coralogix teams: the budget splits, and queries across teams break.

Open with

“Who in your company decides what gets kept, and can they see what the pipeline does today?”

The strongest version of this pain is ownership, not cost. Large accounts have several internal groups funding one observability bill, and none of them can see or control their own share. Let the visitor describe that before you open the builder.

Demo 3 · Click path · 4 minutes

Two use cases win this demo

A · Keep one copy with PII, one without

Add a fan out. On one branch, a parsing step that removes the personal fields, going to the general dataset. On the other, the full event to a security dataset with its own access.

Say: “Same event, two shapes, two sets of permissions, one pipeline.”

B · Give a business unit its own path

Route a branch to that unit’s own pipeline and set a limit on it. Who can edit that limit is still being designed.

Say: “Their own route and their own limit, in one place.”

1

Open the logs pipeline. Say: this is the fixed path every log takes today, drawn for the first time.

2

Add a router, then a fan out. Drag a parse step after the fan out, not before it. Order is yours.

3

Set a quota on one branch, and a destination on the other. Save as a draft.

Confirm each screen in your environment before the show. The flag is traffic-management.

This is a front end. Do not click anything that implies data is flowing through it, and do not volunteer that the backend is not ready. If asked directly whether it is running in production, say it is a design preview and we are taking design partners.

What they will ask

QuestionAnswer

Demo 4 · Virtual datasets · Preview

Splitting your data
breaks everything
that points at it.

What the day looks like now

Datasets give each kind of data its own access, retention and quota. So customers split default logs into real datasets.

Every dashboard, alert and saved query that named the old dataset now points at the wrong place.

And any question that crosses the split date needs a union of the old and the new, written by hand, by someone who remembers the date.

Why the obvious fixes fail

Rewrite everything on the day of the split: a migration project nobody will fund.

Write the union in every query: it works until the next change, and every user has to know the history.

Do not split at all: the data stays in one place, with one set of rules.

Open with

“If you reorganized your data tomorrow, how many dashboards and alerts would you have to rewrite?”

Any customer who has already split data has lived this. Let them describe it.

5

Land the point. Everyone queries one name, and you change the list in one place.

60 second version

“Think of a shortcut. No data is copied, nothing is stored twice, no extra ingest. It is a name over datasets you already have.”

Not in the first build

Keeping an old name alive after a split, filters, name patterns, one virtual dataset inside another. Say “that is where this goes”, no date.

Front end behind a flag from the end of this week. Confirm each screen in your environment. Until the flag is on, use the SE copy of the mockups and stay on what this slide shows.

Say no extra ingest and no extra storage. Do not say free: price and quota handling are not decided. If a visitor asks whether it costs anything, say the data is not copied, so there is no second ingest or storage charge, and the commercial detail is not settled.
