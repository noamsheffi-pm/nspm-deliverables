Quota Flow Map — reservations left, limits right

Left of the divider is a partition. Team quota splits into unit types, unit types into entityType reservations plus an unallocated remainder. Hard guarantees, so the widths sum to exactly 100% at every column.

Right of the divider are ceilings. The TCO column is the aggregate of many TCO rules grouped into High, Med, Low and Block. Datasets sit behind them, and routing is constrained: frequentsearch/<pillar> can only be fed by High, default/<name> only by Med and Low. Every hop on this side compares the sum of its children's ceilings against its own: excess is hatched red, shortfall is a dashed grey gap.

How to read the right-hand side
  1. A priority node is an aggregate, not a rule. High is the sum of every TCO rule that routes to high priority for that entityType, and the rule count is in its tooltip. That aggregation is the backend cost the team flagged on 2026-07-29, and the reason this half needs a TCO rule summary per entityType before it can ship.
  2. Every node's percentage is measured against its own parent, never against a lane total. A dataset reads as a share of the priority band feeding it, a band reads as a share of the entityType reservation. Over-subscription of a whole hop is stated once, on the hatched marker and in validation, so a single dataset can never appear to be 260% of anything on its own.
  3. Routing is a constraint, not a preference. A frequentsearch dataset fed by Med or Low, or a default dataset fed by High, is a plan error and is called out as one. Frequent search is a different dataspace with its own commercial model, so those units are not cost-equivalent to default-dataspace units of the same width.
  4. Block is terminal. Units routed to Block by policy reach no dataset. Deliberate loss, and the only red on this map the customer chose.
