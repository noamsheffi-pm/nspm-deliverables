Coralogix · Monetization

The usage number we sell against should never move because we deployed.

The ask: one usage meter. V2 replaces V1, and V1 gets an end date.

April · Conviva

An alert fires on usage the customer never sent.

Conviva's usage alert goes off. Their traffic did not change. The spike does not match their own Data Usage tab. They open a ticket. Our support engineer looks and writes back:

"That spike is on us, due to a deployment."
May to June · Elsevier, Connecteam, Kotak, Dazn

It is not one customer. It becomes a P0.

Elsevier sees the same spikes across all its applications. More accounts report it, and it becomes P0 BUGV2-6332. We patch it in June. The spikes get rarer. They do not stop, because the old meter recounts the whole day every minute, so any change on our side can double it.

July · Kotak

Then the spike shows up while a customer's data is blocked.

Kotak's Dynamic TCO alert fires on a spike that no graph can show, and their data is blocked. The customer cannot tell what is real usage and what is our meter. The number they use to manage their quota is no longer something they can trust.

September · Euronext

Meanwhile, we start selling AI units, and the meter cannot follow.

AI units are now a separate pool and a separate line on the order form. Euronext has 260 CX + 10 AI units per day. They ask why the unit count differs between screens. The V1 metric mixes everything into one units number: Olly use is missing, and AI evaluations are counted as spans. A customer can see AI units on the usage screen, but cannot put AI use on a dashboard or an alert.

And the next question every large account asks, "which team spent it?", cannot ship on V1. V1 is frozen, because any change to it makes a spike.

Today

V2 is built. It is waiting for one decision, not for engineering.

V2 counts only what was used in each short window, from the same events that feed billing. A change on our side cannot replay the day, so it cannot make a spike. It runs next to V1 on eu2 today, and over 24 hours units differ by less than 0.1% on spans, logs and metrics. It shows AI use separately, and it can carry team and service labels.

The open question is not "can we". It is "do we run one meter or two".
The bet, and how we will know
  • False-spike escalations go to 0 in the quarter after V1 is off.
  • Every account with an AI quota can put AI use on a dashboard or an alert, apart from CX use.
  • Usage by env, type and service ships, instead of waiting on V1.

What it costs: customers rewrite their usage dashboards and alerts once (we ship the new dashboard and a guide; the count of affected accounts is in progress). While both meters run, a customer pays about twice for this metric, so the end date matters. A few V1 views still need a V2 version. Halo and I own those, and none needs a decision from you.

A · recommended

One meter

Set a V1 end date. We move every account and own the migration.

B

Two meters

Keep a reduced V1 next to V2 with no end date. Lower migration cost. Customers keep seeing two numbers, and we support both.

I recommend A. Conviva got "that spike is on us" from our support. Our next answer to customers should be an end date for V1.
